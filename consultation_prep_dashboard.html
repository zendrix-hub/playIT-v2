Anticipated Questions from Your Capstone Teacher / Adviser
Defense Cheat Sheet

Be prepared to answer these questions confidently during your consultation tomorrow:

Q1: "Why did you choose the UPA Model instead of generic ISO 9241-11 or standard TAM?"
Core Framework
Strong Answer:
"Per CIT-U Capstone 2 Educational Systems guidelines, standard corporate models like TAM (Technology Acceptance Model) or ISO 9241-11 are designed for workplace productivity software. They fail to capture two non-negotiable dimensions of pediatric early literacy:
  • Pedagogy (P): A corporate usability test does not evaluate whether letter sounds follow the Marungko sequence, or whether speech models teach pure phonemes vs. letter names.
  • Accessibility (A): Standard usability assumes adult motor control. Grade 1 children require specific pediatric ergonomics—minimum 64dp touch targets, tap-only mechanics (no complex dragging), spoken dual-coding for non-readers, and 100% offline zero-cost equity.
Therefore, we adopted the UPA Framework (Usability, Pedagogy, Accessibility), operationalizing Usability through standard SUS (adults) and the Smileyometer (kids), Pedagogy through a 12-item DepEd Teacher Checklist, and Accessibility through 7 pediatric criteria."
Q2: "Why are you only covering 26 letters when the DepEd Alpabetong Filipino has 28 letters?"
Curricular Scope
Strong Answer:
"PlayIT adapts the Marungko frequency-based progression specifically for Grade 1 English Phonics:
  1. Ñ (En-ye) does not exist in the English language or English phonetic reading instruction.
  2. NG is a voiced velar nasal consonant digraph (/ŋ/), not an isolated alphabetic grapheme. Introducing 'NG' as an isolated single letter in early phonics causes severe phonological confusion when blending simple CVC words (like SAM, CAT, DOG).
  3. ASR Acoustic Stability: Embedded offline Vosk speech recognition handles isolated single English graphemes with high acoustic precision, whereas evaluating children uttering isolated non-English graphemes leads to erratic false-rejections.
All 4 certified DepEd teachers evaluated this and gave 100% consensus approval (PED-02) to our 26-letter, 7-biome, 33-CVC word matrix."
Q3: "Why did Respondent 2 score a 50.0 on the SUS? Did they hate the app?"
Statistical Rigor
Strong Answer:
"No, Respondent 2 actually loved the app verbally! The 50.0 score is a textbook example of survey acquiescence bias:
  • The System Usability Scale (Brooke, 1996) alternates positive (odd) and negative reverse-scored (even) statements.
  • Respondent 2 uniformly selected '5.0' (Strongly Agree) down the entire form without reading the reverse polarity, meaning they marked 5.0 on 'I found the system unnecessarily complex' and 'I found the system very cumbersome.'
  • When scored according to standard Brooke formulas, negative items scored 5 contribute 0 points (5 - 5 = 0), penalizing their score down to 50.0.
  • Our peer respondents scored 77.5, 80.0, 85.0, and 85.0. Excluding this acquiescent outlier yields an adjusted peer Mean SUS of 81.88 / 100 (Grade A-). Even including this outlier, our Mean SUS of 75.50 comfortably exceeds both the industry average (68.0) and our target (75.0)."
Q4: "What is the most critical issue you discovered, and what is your engineering response?"
Critical Insight
Strong Answer:
"The single most valuable finding came from Item PED-08 where certified DepEd educators (specifically Teacher Leony Layaguin and Teacher Joy Flores) flagged that certain phoneme models uttered the letter name or a trailing schwa /ə/ instead of the pure phoneme.

For example, the letter M was uttered as 'ma, ma, ma' rather than pure continuous humming /m/ ('mmm'). In early reading, if a child learns /m/ as 'ma', when they attempt to blend SAM in Chapter 1, they sound out 'ma - a - t' instead of 's - a - m'.

Our Week 3 Engineering Action:
  • Classified as P0 (Immediate Priority) for Week 3.
  • We are updating FR-02 (Hear It Module) in SRS v3.0 to mandate zero syllabic concatenation.
  • Re-mastering all phoneme audio assets via clean acoustic synthesis for pure isolated phonemes.
  • Refactoring AudioPlaybackManager in SDD v2.0 using Android SoundPool for zero-latency instant phoneme audio playback."
